# Nomad Bridge (August 2022)

**Loss:** ~$190M
**Chain:** Ethereum mainnet (Nomad's `Replica` contract; other chains had matching Replicas)
**Root cause:** `_committedRoot` initialized to `0x00`, causing any Merkle proof rooted in `0x00` to verify.
**Attack date:** 2022-08-01

## What happened

Nomad's optimistic bridge relied on a `Replica` contract on each chain that verified messages against a signed Merkle root. During a routine upgrade, `Replica` was reinitialized with `_committedRoot = 0x0000...`. The `acceptableRoot` mapping then had `[0x00] = timestamp`, meaning any message whose Merkle proof hashed to the zero root was accepted as "processed."

The attacker crafted a `Process(message)` call with a bogus proof that hashed to zero and successfully withdrew funds. Once the transaction was seen in the mempool, anyone could copy it, change the destination address, and drain the bridge themselves. The attack became a free-for-all: over 300 EOAs participated in the following hours.

## The on-chain signature opsentry would have caught

The distinguishing pattern was not any single call, it was the **rate**:

- **Dozens of `Process` calls per block** from unrelated `msg.sender` addresses
- **Small amounts per call**, but in aggregate draining hundreds of millions
- All targeting the same `Replica` contract
- All within a short window (~4 hours)

A monitor watching `Process` events with a rate-limit trigger ("more than N per block, or more than M per hour") would have fired on block 15,259,101 (the first hostile Process). At that point human operators could have paused the bridge.

Nomad's specific case also had a stronger signal: **any** `Process` call after the buggy initialization was suspicious, because the bridge had almost no legitimate traffic. But you can't rely on "the bridge is quiet" as a signal for bridges with normal volume; the rate change matters more than absolute rate.

## Ruleset

See [`2022-08-nomad.yaml`](./2022-08-nomad.yaml). It watches Nomad's original Ethereum `Replica` (now defunct) as historical documentation. To adapt for your bridge:

1. Change `address` to your bridge's message-processing contract.
2. Change the event to whatever your bridge emits on successful message processing (e.g. `MessageProcessed`, `Executed`, `Received`).
3. Rate-limit the alert with `receivers[].throttle:` so a legitimate 20-message batch doesn't flood you.
4. Set the alert `severity: critical` and route to a paging receiver, not a log-only one.
