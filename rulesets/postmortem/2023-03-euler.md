# Euler Finance (March 2023)

**Loss:** ~$197M
**Chain:** Ethereum mainnet
**Root cause:** Missing health check on `donateToReserves`
**Attack date:** 2023-03-13
**Attacker was refunded, funds recovered.**

## What happened

Euler's lending pool had a function `donateToReserves` that let a user donate their eToken balance to the pool's reserves. It did not check the donor's account health afterward. The attacker:

1. Deposited collateral, borrowed the maximum against it.
2. Called `donateToReserves` to burn a large amount of their own eTokens, making their equity go deeply negative.
3. Immediately called `liquidate` on themselves through a controlled second account, at a violent 20% discount.
4. Repeated to drain multiple pools.

The whole exploit was a chain of `Deposit → Borrow → Donate → Liquidate` events, all initiated by contracts the attacker owned, all within a small number of blocks.

## The on-chain signature opsentry would have caught

The pattern that makes this different from a legitimate liquidation:

- **Same tx sender** initiates both `donateToReserves` and the follow-up `Liquidation`.
- Or **liquidation of an account that donated to reserves in the previous block**.
- The health-factor drop across the pair of events is disproportionate to normal position management.

A single rule watching `Liquidation` events whose `violator` address appears as `donor` in a `RepayReserves` (a.k.a. donateToReserves) event within the last ~5 blocks would have fired on every step of the attack.

## Ruleset

See [`2023-03-euler.yaml`](./2023-03-euler.yaml). It targets Euler's live pool address (now defunct) as historical documentation. To adapt for your protocol:

1. Change `address` to your lending pool.
2. Change the ABI to your pool's `Liquidation` and reserve-donation event signatures.
3. Adjust the "recent event window" if your protocol's normal block cadence is different.
